Overview

Mooring Lab lets you explore how a tanker, its mooring lines and its berth or buoy respond to wind, current and waves.

It is for technical readers, students and curious engineers learning load paths, compliance, motion and load redistribution. You can change a line, compare a vessel or watch a transient without building a numerical model from scratch.

This is an educational, exploratory tool, not a class-approved design or operational assessment tool. Its illustrative coefficients and geometry cannot establish whether a real mooring is safe.

Quick start

  1. Open the simulator’s Chat/tanker-mooring-simulator/output/index.html in a modern WebGL browser. It needs internet access for pinned three.js 0.160.0, lil-gui 0.19.2 and uPlot 1.6.31 libraries. This wiki itself works entirely offline.
  2. Pick a scenario in the scene toolbar. Calm is useful for orientation; Design storm makes loads and motion visible.
  3. The run starts automatically. Use Pause / Resume (Space) to stop or play; Step advances one fixed step and pauses.
  4. Watch Line watch and the tension chart. Compare kN with % MBL, then inspect the motion and fender charts.

Use the sidebar to read one page at a time. Each page has a shareable hash address; search filters page titles and their contents. Printing includes all eleven pages.

Tanker mooring concepts

Moorings keep a tanker positioned for cargo transfer while allowing limited motion. Excess motion can damage transfer equipment, overload a line, push the hull into fenders or cause successive failures as loads redistribute.

Vessel classes

Common approximate carrying-capacity bands are Aframax 80,000–120,000 DWT, Suezmax 120,000–200,000 DWT and VLCC 200,000–320,000 DWT. These are contextual industry bands, not simulator input ranges; names and boundaries vary. DWT measures carrying capacity, not the vessel’s total floating mass. The preset table gives the exact dimensions and displacements used here.

Berth and single point mooring

At a jetty, multiple lines connect the hull to fixed shore points, and fenders cushion contact. At a catenary anchor leg mooring (CALM) buoy, bow hawsers connect the tanker to a buoy held by seabed chains. The vessel can turn around that connection to align with the resultant environment: weathervaning.

Line roles

  • Head and stern lines lead beyond the bow and stern, restraining combined longitudinal and transverse motion.
  • Breast lines lead approximately across the vessel and restrain sway.
  • Spring lines lead mainly along the vessel and restrain surge.

Fenders provide a compliant normal reaction and some tangential friction. They reduce contact shock; they do not hold a ship against an outward environmental force.

Six degrees of freedom

Loads and margins

Wind acts on the exposed hull and superstructure; current acts on the submerged hull. Waves excite oscillation and a mean drift load. Passing vessels can create a temporary surge and yaw disturbance.

Minimum breaking load (MBL) characterizes line strength. A safe working load (SWL) is an allowable working threshold chosen below breaking strength. Real systems need margin for unequal load sharing, degradation and transients. Here SWL is an adjustable alarm percentage, initially 50% MBL; it is not a safety certification.

Mooring lines in depth

Elastic lines

Elastic lines represent relatively taut berth ropes and hawsers. For endpoint distance d and unstretched length L, strain ε = max(0, d/L − 1). The tension-only law is:

EAₑ = EA × material factor / (1 + 5 × tail)
T = EAₑ ε (1 + 12ε)

Compression produces zero tension. The quadratic term hardens the line as it stretches. Material changes multiply rigidity; they do not automatically change MBL, weight or other properties.

MaterialRigidity factorInterpretation
HMPE2.2High modulus polyethylene; comparatively stiff
Polyester1Reference synthetic rope
Nylon0.35More compliant synthetic rope
Wire1.6Stiff metallic rope
Chain1Typically used in weighted catenary legs

A compliant tail is represented by a scalar stiffness reduction, not a separate segment. Default tail fraction is 0.15 even for chain legs; these are illustrative model settings.

Elastic catenaries

Use catenaries when cable weight and changing suspended geometry matter, particularly buoy anchor chains. With arc coordinate s, horizontal tension H, vertical tension V and submerged weight per metre w:

T(s) = √(H² + V²)
dx/ds = H/T + H/EAₑ
dz/ds = V/T + V/EAₑ
dV/ds = w

The code integrates these relations analytically and solves endpoint geometry with finite-difference Newton updates, up to 25 iterations, stopping at a 0.001 m endpoint residual. A flat-bed branch uses 32 bisection iterations for a grounded-plus-suspended approximation. It omits soil friction and grounded elasticity (while retaining an approximate horizontal elastic extension). Below 0.1 N/m weight the solver falls back to a linear tension-only elastic cable.

The catenary drawing follows an approximate static solved shape; its plotted arc omits elastic extension terms. The elastic-line sag is only a visual approximation. Neither is a dynamic cable model.

Pretension and line handling

Set pretension recalculates L at the current endpoint distance using the elastic hardening law. For catenaries this is not an inverse catenary solution: tune L and inspect actual tension. In Berth, setting pretension restarts the run while preserving line settings and rebalancing initial fender compression.

Winch speed changes L each step: positive pays out, negative pays in, with a 2 m minimum. Brake slip begins above the chosen percentage of MBL; zero turns it off. Payout per step is min(2 m, 2(T − threshold)L Δt/EA), followed by force recomputation. This rate limit does not cap tension or guarantee protection from breakage.

With Break above MBL enabled, T > MBL releases the line and sets its force to zero. Broken / release manually toggles release; clearing it can reconnect the line. There is no physical recoil, fatigue model or quick-release-hook mechanism. The Line failure scenario repeatedly forces its first line released after 15 s.

Fenders

Berth mode creates four fenders at x/L = −0.34, −0.12, 0.12 and 0.34. Initial compression balances the lateral pretension of the current line configuration; symmetric default lines should therefore begin near equilibrium.

δ = max(0, preload + sway + rotated contact y − B/2)
R = max(0, k δⁿ + 250,000 max(0, vcontact))
Ft = −μR tanh(vt / 0.05)
E = k δⁿ⁺¹ / (n + 1)

Use k in N/mⁿ in these equations; the control is in kN/mⁿ. Default k is 1,800 kN/mⁿ and n is 1.8. Contact compression velocity is sway velocity plus yaw rate times the rotated contact x coordinate. The dashpot adds reaction only during compression. Tangential friction uses surge velocity minus yaw rate times contact y, smoothed over 0.05 m/s.

Energy and travel

The area under the elastic reaction curve is stored compression energy E, displayed in kJ. It is not cumulative dissipated impact energy; damping and friction losses are not accumulated in this number.

Compression greater than the travel threshold (default 2 m) raises FENDER TRAVEL EXCEEDED and changes the fender colour. There is no rigid stop, crushing law or berth collision barrier: reaction continues beyond the alarm threshold. A run exceeding travel requires caution, not confidence that the hull has been stopped safely.

Environment model

Directions and relative loads

All direction inputs mean force or wave propagation toward the specified angle: 0° is +x, 90° is +y, 180° is −x and 270° is −y. These are not meteorological “from” directions. Relative angle is direction minus hull yaw. Earth-frame vessel velocity is transformed to body axes before relative flow is computed.

Illustrative OCIMF-style component fits use Fx = ½ρ Afront 0.8 ux|ux| C and Fy = ½ρ Aside uy|uy| C, then rotate back to earth axes. They are not the OCIMF vessel coefficient tables. Air density is 1.225 kg/m³ and water density is 1,025 kg/m³. Wind uses preset windage; current uses L×draft and beam×draft.

The yaw lever is 0.035L sin(2α). Vertical load levers are +0.6 draft for wind and −0.45 draft for current, producing roll/pitch moments as well. Current loads are multiplied by min(3, 1 + 0.3[draft/max(1 m, depth − draft)]¹·²) × (1 − 0.4 profile). This is an approximate UKC amplification and shear reduction, not a resolved depth profile.

Uwind(t) = U [1 + gust(0.6 sin(0.43t) + 0.3 sin(1.17t + 0.7) + 0.1 sin(2.3t))]

Gusts are deterministic. The wave seed does not randomize gusts. The environment chart reports this gusted speed, configured current speed and sea elevation at the earth origin.

JONSWAP sea and excitation

Twenty-four seeded components approximate a JONSWAP spectrum. Hs normalizes their summed variance so Hs = 4√variance; Tp sets peak angular frequency 2π/Tp; gamma sharpens the peak. Frequencies span 0.35–3.05 times the peak frequency in midpoint bins. Directions are sampled uniformly within waveDir ± spread and phases are seeded. Both the physics and ocean shader use the same components.

η(x,y,t) = Σ a cos(k[x cosθ + y sinθ] − ωt + phase)
k = ω²/g

Horizontal wave accelerations use aω² sin(phase) exp(−k draft/2). Fifteen waterplane samples (5 longitudinal × 3 transverse) apply Froude-Krylov-style horizontal acceleration and vertical buoyancy excitation, scaled by waveCoeff. Moments come from each strip’s lever arm. The horizontal excitation uses the code’s ρ × preset displacement / 15 factor; it is an illustrative approximation, not a calibrated displacement-volume model.

Optional mean drift adds ρgHs²B/16 along waveDir. It has no frequency-dependent drift coefficients or direct yaw moment. Wave dispersion remains deep-water even at small water depths.

Passing ship

The Passing ship surge scenario adds a prescribed surge force P = 350,000 exp(−((t−35)/12)²) sin((t−20)0.13) N and yaw moment 0.15LP. Its Gaussian envelope is centered on 35 s; the peak of the signed force need not occur there. There is no moving ship flow solver or editable passing-ship geometry.

Physics model

The hull state q = [surge, sway, heave, roll, pitch, yaw] uses metres and radians internally. Rotated attachment positions use a yaw–pitch–roll matrix. Moments are r × F, but generalized accelerations use diagonal inertia without full gyroscopic or Euler-rate coupling.

Mᵢ q̈ᵢ = Fenv,ᵢ + Flines,ᵢ + Ffenders,ᵢ − Dᵢ − Kᵢqᵢ

Mass, added inertia and restoring

Let m = displacement in tonnes × 1,000 kg/t and a = addedMass. The diagonal effective masses/inertias are:

AxisEffective mass or inertia
Surgem(1 + 0.05a)
Swaym(1 + 0.8a)
Heavem(1 + 0.85a)
Rollm(B² + draft²)/12 × (1 + 0.3a)
PitchmL²/12 × (1 + 0.7a)
Yawm(L² + B²)/12 × (1 + 0.5a)

Heave stiffness is ρgLB × waterplane coefficient; roll stiffness is mgGM and pitch stiffness is mgGML. Surge, sway and yaw have zero hydrostatic stiffness and rely on the mooring system. GM and GML are fixed input stability measures, not recomputed from loading geometry.

Dᵢ = damping × Mᵢ [vᵢ/τᵢ + vᵢ|vᵢ|/bᵢ]

τ = [180, 100, 18, 30, 25, 100] s. The code uses b = 100 for translations and 2 for rotations as illustrative quadratic damping constants in its SI/radian state convention.

CALM buoy

The buoy starts at [L/2 + 70, 0, 0] m, with three translational degrees of freedom. Six chain anchors lie on a 220 m radius at the seabed. Chain forces and equal-and-opposite hawser forces move the buoy. Static vertical chain load is balanced initially; incremental heave restoring is −1,000,000 z N.

Each axis uses effective mass buoyMass × 1,000 + 800,000 kg, linear damping 100,000 v and quadratic relative-current drag buoyDrag × 1,000 urel|urel|. Horizontal current acts on x/y; vertical drag resists vertical velocity. Buoy rotations, explicit wave excitation and direct wind forces on the buoy are absent.

Fixed-step integration

vₙ₊₁ = vₙ + Δt (F − D − Kqₙ)/M
qₙ₊₁ = qₙ + Δt vₙ₊₁

This semi-implicit Euler scheme advances on a fixed Δt, initially 0.02 s. An accumulator receives min(real frame time, 0.1 s) × time scale. Each frame processes at most 200 steps. Remaining backlog above 2 s is capped and increments the compute-budget counter; simulated time slows relative to the requested rate rather than increasing Δt. A non-finite hull state throws an error and pauses the animation loop.

Using the simulator

Drag the 3D view to orbit and scroll to zoom. Camera choices are Free orbit, Top, Side and Bow; Follow starts checked and tracks hull translation. Controls continue below the scene on narrow screens.

Pause / Resume toggles the run; Step pauses and advances one Δt; Reset starts a fresh run retaining configuration but restoring generated line settings. Scenario selection restores all configuration defaults first. No separate Play button exists.

Berth versus CALM SPM

Berth uses six elastic lines and four fenders. SPM uses one or two bow hawsers (two by default), six weighted catenary chain legs and no fenders. Hawsers begin slack in calm water; a stored zero pretension does not impose a tension target during motion. Watch buoy translation, changing line shares and vessel heading as forcing turns the tanker.

Guided experiments

  1. Current and lead direction. Start Calm, set currentDir to 0°, then compare current 0.3 and 0.8 m/s. Each environment edit starts a fresh run. Compare surge offset and spring loads after settling; then repeat at 90° and compare sway and fenders.
  2. Material compliance. Use a modest steady load, save JSON as a baseline and change one breast line from Polyester to Nylon. Keep EA, MBL and L unchanged. Watch its tension and the redistributed loads. The material multiplier changes, not strength; compare after the transient settles.
  3. Release a line. In a modest forced berth run, pause, mark Stern Broken / release, then resume. Compare neighbouring tensions and excursion. Reload the baseline to repeat at smaller Δt.
  4. Storm at berth and SPM. Choose Design storm, record the berth response, then change mode to CALM SPM. The environment stays the same, but the run resets and the mooring layout changes. Compare heading freedom, hawser load and excursion, allowing enough time for transient motion.

All x axes are simulated seconds. Series colours follow the label order in each chart’s legend and hover title; motion and environment plots mix units. Sampling occurs when at least 0.2 simulated seconds have elapsed since the previous sample, so the rate is up to 5 Hz and can be lower due to step alignment. The rolling buffer holds 1,500 rows, approximately five simulated minutes at the maximum sample rate.

Peak excursion is tracked each physics step. Peak tension is updated by HUD calls, normally at sampling and explicit UI updates, so it may miss a tension maximum between samples. Both clear with a fresh run and are not separate CSV columns.

CSV

Export CSV downloads mooring-timeseries.csv containing the current rolling buffer, with five decimal places. Line names replace spaces with underscores. The default berth header is:

time_s,Stern_kN,Aft_breast_kN,Aft_spring_kN,Head_kN,Fore_breast_kN,Fore_spring_kN,surge_m,sway_m,yaw_deg,heave_m,roll_deg,pitch_deg,fender_1_kN,fender_2_kN,fender_3_kN,fender_4_kN,wind_mps,current_mps,wave_m

SPM substitutes Hawser_1_kN (and Hawser_2_kN if present), Chain_1_kN through Chain_6_kN and a single fender_none_kN zero column. Time is seconds; translations and wave elevation are metres; rotations are degrees; tensions/reactions are kN; speeds are m/s. Per-fender energy, compression, line % MBL and state flags are not exported.

JSON save/load

Save JSON downloads mooring-scenario.json with version 1, scenario name, all configuration keys and one settings record per line. Line records retain type, material, MBL, EA, weight, pretension, L, tail, winch, brake and broken. Names, attachment geometry, solved forces and current hull/buoy motion are regenerated.

Loading starts a new run. It does not resume time, history, peaks, camera, follow or paused state from the file. Broken flags and paid-out lengths can survive saving. Missing configuration keys use defaults and unknown keys are ignored. Unknown scenario names fall back to Calm, while the loaded configuration remains.

The validator requires version 1, a configuration object and a line array, checks known configuration value types and finite numbers, checks vessel/condition/mode choices and numeric supported bounds, and requires integer hawserCount. Expected line count is six at berth or six plus hawserCount in SPM. Each line needs allowed type/material, a boolean broken flag and finite numeric settings within the reference-table bounds. Numeric values need not match slider increments; seed need not be an integer.

One validation detail differs from the GUI: JSON depth must be at least the loaded preset draft + 2 m, even for ballast; the GUI uses ceil(active model draft + 2). A shallow ballast configuration saved by the UI can therefore be rejected on reload. Errors produce a “Scenario not loaded” alert.

Assumptions and limits

The model structure follows established mooring concepts, but its values are illustrative. It is not calibrated to a specific ship, rope, fender, terminal or operating procedure.

  • Hull mass, added mass and damping are diagonal approximations. There is no radiation memory, diffraction, measured response-amplitude operator (RAO), full rigid-body gyroscopic coupling or frequency-dependent hydrodynamics.
  • Hydrostatic restoring is linear in heave, roll and pitch. Waterplane area, GM and GML do not update with immersion or heel. Large rotations invalidate that approximation and the simplified angular integration.
  • Wind/current loads use simple component drag fits and empirical levers. Gusts and current shear are prescribed reductions, not turbulent flow fields. UKC amplification is capped and does not resolve shallow-water flow.
  • Waves use 24 components and deep-water dispersion at any depth. Excitation is a coarse 15-strip approximation with a simplified mean drift load. The passing-ship disturbance is a prescribed pulse.
  • Lines are massless quasi-static force elements. There is no bending, cable inertia, dynamic touchdown, snap-load resolution, soil friction, abrasion, fatigue, creep or strength degradation. Material/tail stiffness multipliers do not represent full rope construction.
  • Brake slip is rate limited, not an ideal tension limiter. Winch drive power and control systems are absent. Release has no recoil simulation. Line settings can create unrealistic geometry.
  • Fenders have an unbounded power-law curve plus damping/friction, no rigid contact stop, damage or certified performance curve. Stored energy is not accumulated dissipation.
  • The buoy translates only, with simple added mass, drag and linear heave stiffness. There are no buoy rotations or detailed buoy hydrostatics.
  • Stiff short lines, severe forcing and large edits can outrun the fixed-step integrator’s accuracy or stability. Compute-budget slowing preserves the selected step, not numerical correctness.

Checking a run

  1. Check Calm equilibrium before applying forcing. Default symmetric berth lines should balance fender preload; calm SPM should have slack hawsers and balanced chains.
  2. Use a steady current and allow settling. Mean load should be balanced by mooring/fender forces, with damping acting during motion. Low damping can require long runs.
  3. Inspect excursions, individual lines and fender compression together. Check SWL, breaking and travel alarms; absence of an alarm does not validate the model.
  4. Repeat the same settings and seed at a smaller fixed step and compare CSV trends. Reset after extreme edits. If peaks, offsets or stability depend strongly on step size, do not interpret the original result.
  5. Use measured site/vessel data, validated specialist software and a qualified marine engineer for real design or operations.

Glossary

Terms describe general mooring concepts; implementation details refer specifically to Mooring Lab.

Added mass
Extra effective inertia associated with accelerating surrounding water. Here it is represented by fixed diagonal multipliers.
Aframax
A tanker category commonly around 80,000–120,000 DWT. The model preset is 110,000 DWT with 132,000 tonnes displacement.
Ballast
A loading condition with less cargo and ballast water used for stability and trim. The simulator applies fixed draft, mass and windage multipliers.
Bollard
A fixed shore fitting around which mooring lines are secured. Shore endpoints here are fixed points, without fitting strength checks.
Brake holding capacity
The load a winch brake can hold before slipping. Here the editable brake threshold is a percentage of MBL, with rate-limited payout.
Breast line
A line leading mainly transversely from ship to shore, restraining sway.
CALM buoy
Catenary anchor leg mooring buoy: a floating connection held by seabed chain legs. The tanker connects through bow hawsers.
Catenary
The hanging shape of a weighted flexible cable. The simulator includes elastic extension and an approximate flat-bed contact branch.
Compliant tail
A more extensible section added to a stiff line to increase compliance. Here it reduces effective EA through a scalar factor.
Current coefficient
A dimensionless scaling of hydrodynamic current loads. The simulator uses illustrative body-axis drag fits.
Damping
Forces or moments opposing motion. Linear and quadratic terms reduce hull oscillation in the model.
Directional spreading
Variation of wave-component directions about a mean direction. Here components are uniformly sampled within a specified half-width.
Displacement
Total floating vessel mass, equal to the mass of displaced water. Preset displacement in tonnes is converted to hull mass in kilograms.
DWT
Deadweight tonnage: carrying capacity including cargo, fuel, water and stores. It is distinct from displacement.
EA
Axial rigidity: elastic modulus E times cross-sectional area A, measured in newtons. Higher EA generally means less extension for a given tension.
Fender
A compliant contact element between vessel and berth. Its reaction resists compression and its friction resists sliding.
Froude-Krylov force
Force from incident-wave pressure acting on the hull, before diffraction corrections. Mooring Lab uses a coarse strip approximation.
GM
Transverse metacentric height, a small-angle roll-stability measure in metres. The roll restoring coefficient here is mgGM.
GML
Longitudinal metacentric height, used for pitch stability. The pitch restoring coefficient here is mgGML.
Gust
A variation of wind speed with time. The simulator combines three deterministic sine functions.
Hawser
A heavy mooring rope. In SPM mode one or two elastic hawsers connect the bow to the buoy.
Head line
A line led from the forward ship toward a shore point beyond the bow. It contributes longitudinal and transverse restraint.
Heave
Vertical hull translation along z. Positive heave is upward.
HMPE
High modulus polyethylene, a high-stiffness synthetic fibre. Its model rigidity multiplier is 2.2.
Hs
Significant wave height in metres. The generated component variance is normalized to Hs = 4√variance.
JONSWAP
A peaked wind-wave spectral shape. Gamma controls peak enhancement in this simulator.
MBL
Minimum breaking load, a line-strength reference. Optional automatic release occurs above this load in the model.
Mean drift force
A non-oscillatory wave load. Here it is approximated as proportional to Hs squared.
Newton iteration
An iterative root-solving method using local derivatives. The catenary solver uses finite-difference derivatives to match endpoints.
OCIMF
Oil Companies International Marine Forum, publisher of Mooring Equipment Guidelines. Its vessel-specific coefficient tables are not implemented here.
Pitch
Rotation about y, following the right-hand rule. Positive pitch lowers the initially +x bow.
Polyester
A synthetic rope material used as the reference stiffness multiplier of 1 in the model.
Pretension
Initial tensile loading applied before environmental forcing. Here an elastic inverse law adjusts unstretched length when Set pretension is edited.
Quick-release hook
A mooring fitting designed to release a line rapidly. The simulator has a release toggle but does not model the mechanism.
RAO
Response-amplitude operator: a frequency-dependent relationship between waves and vessel motion. Measured RAOs are not used here.
Roll
Rotation about the longitudinal x axis. Positive roll lifts the initial +y side.
SPM
Single point mooring, allowing the vessel to turn around a main connection. The implemented option uses a CALM buoy.
Spring line
A line with a strong longitudinal lead that restrains surge. It need not behave as a literal mechanical spring.
Stern line
A line led from the aft ship to a shore point beyond the stern. It supplies combined longitudinal and lateral restraint.
Suezmax
A tanker category commonly around 120,000–200,000 DWT. The simulator preset is 160,000 DWT.
Surge
Hull translation along x, initially along the ship and berth.
Sway
Hull translation along y. Positive sway is toward the berth.
SWL
Safe working load, a working threshold below breaking load. The model alarm defaults to 50% of MBL.
Time scale
Requested ratio of simulated to real elapsed time. Compute limits can prevent the requested speed from being reached.
Touchdown point
Where a suspended cable first meets the seabed. Its movement here is quasi-static, without cable inertia.
Tp
Spectral peak wave period in seconds. The peak angular frequency is 2π/Tp.
UKC
Under-keel clearance, water depth minus vessel draft. The current-load multiplier increases as this gap shrinks.
VLCC
Very large crude carrier, commonly around 200,000–320,000 DWT. The model preset is 300,000 DWT.
Waterplane
The hull area intersecting the mean water surface. The model approximates it as length × beam × waterplane coefficient.
Weathervaning
Turning around a mooring connection to align with the resultant environmental load. The SPM layout permits this heading freedom.
Winch
Equipment for hauling in, paying out and holding a line. Here a prescribed speed changes unstretched length.
Windage
Area exposed to wind. The simulator stores separate side and frontal projected areas.
Yaw
Rotation about vertical z. Positive yaw rotates the initial +x bow toward +y.
